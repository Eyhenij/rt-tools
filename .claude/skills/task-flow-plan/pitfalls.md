## Pitfalls

The traps of the course of work, this part included, stand in the cold part of `task-flow`, its
`pitfalls.md`. The traps of a check and of the state line stand here.

- **A check of absence is read by its hits, not by its number.** While the plan is written the search
  runs with `-n`, and every hit is read: a word the check wants gone also stands in a live article about
  something else. The check is narrowed to the spot; a live article is never renamed to make a check
  pass.
- **A phrase a check looks for stands on one line of the file.** `grep` reads by lines, and a formatted
  text breaks a sentence anywhere. The check asks for two or three words the writer keeps together, or
  reads lines joined by `tr -s '[:space:]' ' '`.
- **A check the plan got wrong is replaced, not worked around.** An approved plan is not edited: the
  replacement goes into the progress as a line «Check of step N.M replaced» with the new call, its
  expectation and the owner's word, in the form of the pattern `task-flow-resume`. Nobody reshapes the work to fit a check that measures something else, and
  nobody marks the step by hand.
- **The edit that moves the state goes alone, before the edits that need it.** Calls of one message are
  judged apart, each against the tree of that minute. Twenty refusals in a row came on edits sent in
  one message with the edit of the state line that lets them through.
